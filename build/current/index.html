Understanding Electric Current Conversions

The standard SI base unit of electric current is the ampere (A), defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the elementary charge e to be 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ coulombs per second.

Supported Electric Current Units (8 Units)

  • Ampere (A) (Base SI unit of electric current (1 C/s)) — Primary SI / Base Reference Unit
  • Milliampere (mA) (Electronics & microcontroller circuits) — 1 mA = 0.001 A
  • Microampere (µA) (Low-power standby & IoT sensors (10⁻⁶ A)) — 1 µA = 0.000001 A
  • Kiloampere (kA) (Power grids & lightning discharges) — 1 kA = 1000 A
  • Ampere-hour (Ah) (Battery capacity over 1 hour (3,600 C)) — 1 Ah = 1 A
  • Milliampere-hour (mAh) (Phone, laptop & power bank battery capacity) — 1 mAh = 0.001 A
  • Abampere (Biot) (abA) (10 Amperes (Electromagnetic CGS unit)) — 1 abA = 10 A
  • Statampere (statA) (Electrostatic CGS unit (~0.334 nA)) — 1 statA = 0 A

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions and verified calculations for electric current units.

How do you convert mAh (milliampere-hours) to Ah (ampere-hours)?

To convert mAh to Ah, divide the mAh rating by 1,000. For example, a 5,000 mAh smartphone battery or power bank has a capacity of 5 Ah (5,000 ÷ 1,000 = 5 Ah).

What is the difference between Amperes (A) and Ampere-hours (Ah)?

Amperes (A) measure instantaneous electric current (the rate of electron flow), while Ampere-hours (Ah) measure total electric charge storage or battery capacity over time (1 Ah = 3,600 coulombs).

How many milliamperes are in 1 Ampere?

There are exactly 1,000 milliamperes (mA) in 1 Ampere (A). Microcontrollers like Arduino typically consume between 20 mA and 50 mA during normal operation.
