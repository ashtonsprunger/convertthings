Understanding Torque Conversions

The standard SI unit of torque is the newton-meter (N·m), defined as the moment resulting from a one-newton force applied perpendicularly to a one-meter moment arm.

Supported Torque Units (6 Units)

  • Newton-meter (N·m) (SI metric standard) — Primary SI / Base Reference Unit
  • Pound-foot (lb-ft) (US automotive lug nuts & engine specs) — 1 lb-ft = 1.355818 N·m
  • Pound-inch (lb-in) (Bicycle torque & small fasteners) — 1 lb-in = 0.112985 N·m
  • Kilogram-force Meter (kgf·m) (European & Japanese legacy specs (9.807 N·m)) — 1 kgf·m = 9.80665 N·m
  • Kilonewton-meter (kN·m) (Heavy industrial, wind turbines & structural) — 1 kN·m = 1000 N·m
  • Ounce-force Inch (oz-in) (Stepper motors, RC servos & robotics) — 1 oz-in = 0.007062 N·m

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions and verified calculations for torque units.

How do you convert Newton-meters (N·m) to foot-pounds (ft-lb)?

Multiply the torque value in N·m by 0.737562 (or divide by 1.355818). For example, 100 N·m is equal to approximately 73.76 lb-ft.

How do you convert foot-pounds (ft-lb) to Newton-meters (N·m)?

Multiply the torque value in ft-lb by 1.355818. For example, 100 ft-lb of automotive lug nut torque equals approximately 135.58 N·m.

What is the difference between ft-lb (foot-pound) and in-lb (inch-pound)?

1 foot-pound equals exactly 12 inch-pounds. Inch-pounds are typically used for precision fasteners on bicycles, electronics, and delicate automotive valve covers, while foot-pounds are used for wheel lug nuts and heavy machinery.
