Understanding Energy Conversions

The SI unit of energy is the joule (J). 1 food calorie (kcal) equals 1,000 small gram-calories, or exactly 4,184 joules.

Supported Energy Units (11 Units)

  • Joule (J) — Primary SI / Base Reference Unit
  • Kilojoule (kJ) (Kilojoules) — 1 kJ = 1000 J
  • Calorie (Gram/Small) (cal) — 1 cal = 4.184 J
  • Kilocalorie (Food Calorie) (kcal) — 1 kcal = 4184 J
  • Watt-hour (Wh) — 1 Wh = 3600 J
  • Kilowatt-hour (kWh) — 1 kWh = 3600000 J
  • Megawatt-hour (MWh) (Commercial battery & grid storage (1,000 kWh)) — 1 MWh = 3600000000 J
  • Electronvolt (eV) (Electronvolts (Particle physics)) — 1 eV = 0 J
  • British Thermal Unit (BTU) — 1 BTU = 1055.055853 J
  • Therm (thm) (US natural gas utility billing (100,000 BTU)) — 1 thm = 105505585.262 J
  • Foot-pound (ft⋅lb) (Foot-pounds (Mechanical work)) — 1 ft⋅lb = 1.355818 J

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions and verified calculations for energy units.

What is the difference between a calorie and a kilocalorie (kcal)?

A small calorie (cal) heats 1 gram of water by 1°C. A dietary calorie (food calorie / Calorie / kcal) equals 1,000 small calories or 4.184 kJ.

How many joules are in a kilowatt-hour?

There are exactly 3,600,000 joules (3.6 megajoules) in 1 kilowatt-hour of electrical energy.
