Understanding Illuminance Conversions

The SI unit of illuminance is the lux (lx), which equals one lumen per square meter (1 lx = 1 lm/m²). It quantifies the intensity of light falling on a given surface.

Supported Illuminance Units (5 Units)

  • Lux (lx) (SI standard illuminance (1 lumen/m²)) — Primary SI / Base Reference Unit
  • Foot-candle (fc) (US architectural & photography lighting (1 lm/ft²)) — 1 fc = 10.76391 lx
  • Phot (ph) (CGS optical standard (10,000 lux / 1 lm/cm²)) — 1 ph = 10000 lx
  • Nox (nox) (Night vision & astronomy optics (1 millilux)) — 1 nox = 0.001 lx
  • Kilolux (klx) (Direct sunlight & greenhouse horticulture) — 1 klx = 1000 lx

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions and verified calculations for illuminance units.

How do you convert foot-candles (fc) to lux (lx)?

Multiply the foot-candle value by 10.7639. For example, standard office lighting of 50 fc equals approximately 538.2 lux (50 × 10.7639 ≈ 538 lx).

What is the difference between a foot-candle and a lux?

Both measure illuminance (light falling on a surface). Lux is the metric SI unit (1 lumen per square meter), while foot-candle is the imperial US customary unit (1 lumen per square foot). Because 1 square meter is approximately 10.764 square feet, 1 fc ≈ 10.764 lux.

What are typical real-world light levels in lux?

Direct bright sunlight: 32,000 to 100,000 lux; Overcast day: 1,000 to 10,000 lux; Office or classroom work: 300 to 500 lux; Home living room: 100 to 200 lux; Full moon at night: 0.1 to 0.3 lux.
