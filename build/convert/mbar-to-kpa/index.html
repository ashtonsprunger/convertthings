Unit Definitions & Metrology Context

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa). Standard sea-level atmosphere is defined as 101,325 Pa (1.01325 bar or ~14.696 psi).

About Millibars (mbar): Millibar (mbar) is a unit in the Pressure measurement category. It is widely used in millibars (barometric pressure). Relative to the pressure base unit, 1 mbar equals 100 Pascals (Pa).

About Kilopascals (kPa): Kilopascal (kPa) is a unit in the Pressure measurement category. It is a standard everyday unit of pressure measurement. Relative to the pressure base unit, 1 kPa equals 1000 Pascals (Pa).

Frequently Asked Questions

Verified answers and formulas for converting millibars to kilopascals.

How many kilopascals are in 1 millibar?

There are 0.1 kilopascals (kPa) in 1 millibar (mbar). Conversely, 1 kilopascal equals 10 millibars.

How do you convert millibars to kilopascals?

To convert millibars (mbar) to kilopascals (kPa), use the formula: kPa = mbar / 10. Divide the millibar value by 10. For example, 10 mbar equals 1 kPa.

How do I convert back from kilopascals to millibars?

To convert in the reverse direction from kilopascals (kPa) to millibars (mbar), use the inverse formula: mbar = kPa × 10. Multiply the kilopascal value by 10 (1 kPa = 10 mbar).

What is the difference between millibar (mbar) and kilopascal (kPa)?

Both millibar and kilopascal measure pressure. Millibar (mbar) is a unit in the Pressure measurement category. It is widely used in millibars (barometric pressure). Relative to the pressure base unit, 1 mbar equals 100 Pascals (Pa). Kilopascal (kPa) is a unit in the Pressure measurement category. It is a standard everyday unit of pressure measurement. Relative to the pressure base unit, 1 kPa equals 1000 Pascals (Pa).

How many PSI is in 1 bar?

1 bar equals approximately 14.5038 PSI. For car tires, 2.2 bar is roughly 32 PSI.
