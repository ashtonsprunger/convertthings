Unit Definitions & Metrology Context

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa). Standard sea-level atmosphere is defined as 101,325 Pa (1.01325 bar or ~14.696 psi).

About Pascals (Pa): Pascal (Pa) is a unit in the Pressure measurement category. It is widely used in base si unit (n/m²). The pascal (Pa) serves as the primary reference base unit for the pressure domain.

About Kilopascals (kPa): Kilopascal (kPa) is a unit in the Pressure measurement category. It is a standard everyday unit of pressure measurement. Relative to the pressure base unit, 1 kPa equals 1000 Pascals (Pa).

Frequently Asked Questions

Verified answers and formulas for converting pascals to kilopascals.

How many kilopascals are in 1 pascal?

There are 0.001 kilopascals (kPa) in 1 pascal (Pa). Conversely, 1 kilopascal equals 1000 pascals.

How do you convert pascals to kilopascals?

To convert pascals (Pa) to kilopascals (kPa), use the formula: kPa = Pa / 1000. Divide the pascal value by 1000. For example, 10 Pa equals 0.01 kPa.

How do I convert back from kilopascals to pascals?

To convert in the reverse direction from kilopascals (kPa) to pascals (Pa), use the inverse formula: Pa = kPa × 1000. Multiply the kilopascal value by 1000 (1 kPa = 1000 Pa).

What is the difference between pascal (Pa) and kilopascal (kPa)?

Both pascal and kilopascal measure pressure. Pascal (Pa) is a unit in the Pressure measurement category. It is widely used in base si unit (n/m²). It is a standard everyday unit of pressure measurement. Relative to the pressure base unit, 1 kPa equals 1000 Pascals (Pa).

How many PSI is in 1 bar?

1 bar equals approximately 14.5038 PSI. For car tires, 2.2 bar is roughly 32 PSI.
