Scientific Precision Across 22 Measurement Domains

ConvertThings is a comprehensive, real-time unit conversion platform built for engineers, scientists, students, tradespeople, and home bakers. Every conversion factor is calibrated against official NIST Special Publication 811 (National Institute of Standards and Technology) and ISO/IEC 80000 international metrology definitions, with custom IEEE 754 floating-point normalization that eliminates binary rounding errors.

In addition to standard decimal conversions, ConvertThings supports compound measurements (such as 5 ft 10 in to cm or 2 lbs 4 oz to g), carpenter tape measure fractions (1/16″ ± 1/32″ ruler markings), and a dedicated Kitchen & Cooking Converter that breaks odd fractional cups and spoons into practical measuring drawer tools.

Frequently Asked Questions

Verified metrology standards, fraction handling, and culinary measurements.

How accurate are the unit conversions on ConvertThings?

Every conversion factor on ConvertThings is calibrated against official NIST Special Publication 811 (National Institute of Standards and Technology) and ISO/IEC 80000 metrology constants. Our custom formatting engine also eliminates IEEE 754 binary floating-point rounding artifacts.

How do I convert compound measurements like 5 ft 10 in or 2 lbs 4 oz?

You can type compound measurements directly into either the main input field or the universal search bar (press "/" to focus). For example, typing "5'10\" to cm" or "2 lbs 4 oz to g" resolves both components automatically.

How do kitchen and baking fraction conversions work?

In Kitchen Mode (Cooking), ConvertThings automatically formats odd fractional volumes into practical measuring cups and spoons found in standard home drawers—for example, breaking 13/16 cup into 3/4 cup + 1 tablespoon, or 34 mL into 2 tablespoons + 1 teaspoon.

How do tape measure fraction conversions work?

When converting metric units (millimeters or centimeters) to inches, or when selecting Tape Measure mode (1/16" ± 1/32"), ConvertThings rounds to the nearest practical carpenter ruler mark and indicates any sub-32nd tolerance offset.
