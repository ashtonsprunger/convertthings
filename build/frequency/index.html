Understanding Frequency Conversions

The standard SI unit of frequency is the hertz (Hz), defined as one complete cycle or oscillation per second (1 s⁻¹).

Supported Frequency Units (8 Units)

  • Hertz (Hz) (Base SI unit (1 cycle/sec)) — Primary SI / Base Reference Unit
  • Kilohertz (kHz) (Audio & AM radio (1,000 Hz)) — 1 kHz = 1000 Hz
  • Megahertz (MHz) (FM radio, clock frequencies (10^6 Hz)) — 1 MHz = 1000000 Hz
  • Gigahertz (GHz) (CPUs, Wi-Fi & 5G cellular (10^9 Hz)) — 1 GHz = 1000000000 Hz
  • Terahertz (THz) (Infrared & spectroscopy (10^12 Hz)) — 1 THz = 1000000000000 Hz
  • Revolutions per Minute (RPM) (Motor & engine rotation) — 1 RPM = 0.016667 Hz
  • Radians per Second (rad/s) (Angular velocity (ω = 2πf)) — 1 rad/s = 0.159155 Hz
  • Degrees per Second (°/s) (Gyroscopes & rotational tracking) — 1 °/s = 0.002778 Hz

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions and verified calculations for frequency units.

How do you convert RPM to Hertz (Hz)?

To convert RPM (revolutions per minute) to Hertz (cycles per second), divide by 60. For example, a car engine idling at 600 RPM is spinning at 10 Hz (600 ÷ 60 = 10 Hz).

How many megahertz are in a gigahertz?

There are exactly 1,000 megahertz (MHz) in 1 gigahertz (GHz). For example, a 3.5 GHz CPU processor clock runs at 3,500 MHz.

What is the relationship between frequency and angular velocity?

Angular velocity (ω, in radians per second) is related to cyclic frequency (f, in Hertz) by the formula: ω = 2πf. To convert rad/s to Hz, divide by 2π (approx. 6.283185).
