Ingredient Density & Culinary Weight Conversions

Standard volume converters treat 1 cup of every ingredient the same, but in real-world baking, volume and weight depend on bulk density. 1 US cup (236.59 mL) of water weighs 236.6 grams, while 1 US cup of spooned-and-leveled all-purpose flour weighs 120 grams, granulated white sugar weighs 200 grams, packed brown sugar weighs 213 grams, and US butter weighs 226.8 grams (113.4 grams per stick).

The ConvertThings Kitchen Studio calibrates volume-to-mass conversions against authoritative culinary standards from King Arthur Baking and USDA FoodData Central, and breaks awkward fractional volumes (like 1/3 cup or 34 mL) into practical measuring drawer tools (5 tbsp + 1 tsp).

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about baking weight conversions and kitchen measuring tools.

How many grams is 1 cup of all-purpose flour?

1 US cup of all-purpose flour equals 120 grams when measured with the standard spoon-and-level method (or ~125g if lightly dipped).

How many grams is 1/2 cup of flour?

1/2 cup of all-purpose flour equals exactly 60 grams.

How many cups is 250 grams of flour?

250 grams of all-purpose flour is approximately 2.08 cups (or about 2 cups plus 1 tablespoon).

Why should I weigh flour in grams instead of using cups?

A cup of flour can weigh anywhere from 110g to 150g depending on humidity, brand, and how tightly packed it is. Weighing in grams ensures exact baking ratios every time.
